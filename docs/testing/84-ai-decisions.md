# Strategic and tactical AI contracts

The `game.ai.*` suite includes twenty-nine decision and memory cases for [the AI behavior baseline](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/84), in addition to four robot-definition checks. They call production algorithms and use generated geometry where a decision needs positions, visibility or navigation. They do not replace the AI or change its priorities. The complete catalog remains open: the uncovered scenarios below are baseline blockers.

```powershell
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Debug -Filter '^game\.ai\.'
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Release -Filter '^game\.ai\.'
```

## What the tests establish

| Catalog | Executable cases | Assertions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-01: strategic choices | `action_priorities`, `action_ties` | `CompareAction` ranks attack above forward/capture/intercept, retreat above capture/intercept and capture above forward. Waiting for a team union changes defence-versus-attack preference; already fighting changes it back. Forward candidates prefer shorter region paths. `BestAction` retains the first candidate on an attack tie, and an empty list remains empty |
| AI-01: grouping | `regroup_thresholds`, `regroup_teams_and_actions` | Production `Regroup` merges distinct groups only below 300 world units, keeps an existing group together through 400 units and splits above that distance. Different teams do not merge. A nearby group with a real action transfers that action to the merged group when the other action is None |
| AI-01: integrated strategy | `strategic_tick` | Twenty 10 ms map frames run `TaktHL` and `TaktTL` for the computer side. Its two unarmed robots share team/group membership, choose Defence in region 0 and receive movement-cell destinations (8, 8) and (48, 8). Both RNG modes use seed 79 |
| AI-01: representative member loss | `strategic_member_loss` | After twenty map frames establish the two-robot computer Defence group, production deletion removes one member. Another twenty frames retain both player robots and the computer survivor, refresh the team/group count to one, preserve team 0 and Defence in region 0, and retain the survivor's movement-cell destination (48, 8). There is no separate leader field in this fixture |
| AI-01: nearest-base retirement | `strategic_base_loss` | Direct `TaktHL` observes two friendly bases in different regions. Deleting the nearer base changes the nearest-base region from 1 to 0; deleting the remaining base produces -1. Both robots remain counted, balances stay zero and no construction is queued |
| AI-01: strategic expansion | `strategic_forward`, `strategic_objective_ties`, `strategic_objective_replacement` | `TaktHL` itself generates Forward toward a reachable neutral factory and builds the two-region road path. A wheel-blocked region edge produces Defence at home. With equally distant neutral objectives in two adjacent regions, reversing the adjacency order reverses the chosen objective and its route. Removing a remote objective alone preserves Forward; a new local neutral factory changes the plan to Capture, and deleting that local objective returns it to Defence |
| AI-01: empty-team redistribution | `strategic_empty_team_split` | At an initial map clock of 3,000 ms, the computer keeps its donor team intact. At 3,001 ms, a safe Capture team transfers half its robots to the first empty team; a donor with two separated groups instead transfers the complete first group. The changed members lose their old logic-group IDs pending regrouping. Assertions use actual unit membership, not stale post-transfer team counters |
| AI-01: threat ranking | `threat_priorities` | A configured enemy cannon contributes strength 100 through `PGCalcStat`. `CompareAction` changes defence/attack, defence/forward and defence/capture preferences around team strength 100; defence/retreat changes at 50. Attack and forward beat retreat above the 80% threat boundary and lose below it. The war flag overrides the tested defence/forward, defence/capture and defence/retreat choices |
| AI-01 / AI-03: automatic objectives | `automatic_attack_route`, `automatic_capture_route`, `automatic_defence_route` | The production target searches generate a two-region path and a road route. A wheel restriction prevents cross-region attack and capture. Capture prefers a local factory over a remote neutral factory, including when the connecting edge is blocked. Defence seeks a remote enemy only when a friendly asset in its region needs protection. Deleting the attack target and running `TaktPL` retires its group reference; another search chooses the survivor and rebuilds the path |
| AI-01 / ECO-01: production decisions | `production_wait_choice`, `production_wait_limits`, `production_unaffordable` | Real parsed designs cost 120 and 180 of each resource. With 150 available, `BuildRobot` chooses to wait for the stronger design instead of buying the weaker one, consuming one RNG draw in both modes. A repeated call preserves that plan without another draw. With 100 available, an existing weak-design plan remains within the ten-second income horizon; an existing strong-design plan is canceled. Unaffordable designs cause no purchase, resource debit or random draw |
| AI-02: target memory | `enemy_memory`, `memory_target_retirement` | Slow acquisition deduplicates observations. Enemy-kind lookup respects insertion order. Two missed observations retain a target; a new observation resets the miss count; the third consecutive miss retires it and clears current target references. Production object deletion removes the robot from another robot's enemy list and clears its current target/attack pointers |
| AI-02: reconsideration memory | `ignore_expiry`, `bad_positions` | An ignored target remains ignored at 4,990 ms and expires at 5,000 ms. Re-adding refreshes its timestamp; acquiring it removes the ignore entry. Bad places and coordinates deduplicate and retain entries through their declared capacities |
| AI-02: tactical choice and player intent | `tactical_target_choice`, `tactical_blocked_line` | Production `FirePL` chooses the first equal-distance, unobstructed enemy, then chooses the surviving candidate after target deletion. A friendly robot blocking one equal-distance line causes the open candidate to be preferred; when it is removed from consideration, the blocked candidate remains a valid fallback target. The player group's MoveTo intention remains unchanged. The robots are unarmed, so the checks explicitly require no firing |
| AI-02: radio observations | `radio_group`, `radio_team_region`, `radio_filters` | `GatherInfo(1)` shares observations within a group even across teams and regions. Different groups share only when both team and region match. Friendly observations and sightings already marked for slow retirement do not propagate. A fresh sighting removes an ignore entry, deduplicates the target and resets the receiver's miss count. The existing player MoveTo intention remains unchanged |
| AI-02 / AI-03: order interruption | `interruptibility` | A human-controlled side may interrupt an ordinary capture order, whereas a computer robot may not. AUTOMATIC_MODE alone does not change this condition; FULLAUTO does. Spawn, base move-out and base capture states cannot be interrupted. Removing the capture order restores ordinary interruptibility |
| AI-03: automatic reinforcement gate | `automatic_reinforcement_gate` | A disabled map percentage, nonzero cooldown or ordinary player mode consumes no reinforcement chance draw. Automatic mode at zero cooldown consumes one chance draw per side call, including when no eligible building exists. Seed 79 enters the building scan on its first draw in both RNG modes. No carrier appears; FULLAUTO alone does not enable this scan, and the side method does not decrement the map cooldown |

## Fixture boundaries

The shared `Session` retains its wheel-only synthetic map, active sides, fake audio service and storage-only D3D device. It uses seed 79, controlled map time and the declared no-draw floating-point profile. The integrated strategy case runs both RNG modes at 10 ms; it is not a resource-backed campaign or a native rendering check.

The priority tests populate a public candidate list and call the real comparison and reduction methods. `TaktHL` first initializes the real regional observations where they are needed. This characterizes the ranking contract without pretending that the fixture generated its candidates through a complete strategic expansion. The grouping tests establish initial positions, team IDs and group IDs before calling `Regroup`; they do not teleport robots during a battle. They assert membership and retained action, not the validity of every cached group count.

The new regional cases partition the synthetic movement grid into left and right regions before allocating side observations. Four existing navigation places are assigned to those regions. The objective cases additionally create a four-junction, four-road graph with two equal routes between them. They call `PGFindAttackTarget`, `PGFindCaptureFactory` and `PGFindDefenceTarget` directly and assert the selected object, region sequence and road junctions. They do not advance a multi-region battle or demonstrate physical arrival, capture or combat. Factory and cannon records provide only the ownership, position, state and configured strength consumed by these algorithms; no building or turret geometry is loaded. The radio cases use initial sightings in `CInfo`; production `GatherInfo(1)` decides whether another robot can receive them. Visual acquisition through `GatherInfo(0)` remains uncovered.

The additional strategic cases run both RNG modes with seed 79. Member retirement uses complete production map frames; base and objective cases call `TaktHL` without ticking their mesh-free building records. Bases have zero funds and no turret slots. Nearest-base selection is the current geometric region-center observation, not a guarantee of a traversable escape route. Objective ties use three regions connected as a star with two equal-length roads; each leaf contains one neutral factory. These checks establish strategic planning, not physical capture or arrival. The objective-removal case preserves the existing distinction between a region-level Forward plan and a live Capture opportunity.

The empty-team timer case uses a test-only initial clock input rather than simulating three seconds of prior gameplay. `Profile.initial_time_ms` defaults to zero, accepts 0 through 60,000, and is appended after the existing aggregate fields. A test-only map subclass sets the protected clock during setup and is destroyed through its exact derived type; subsequent time advances still run the production map tick. Nonzero offsets appear explicitly in scenario metadata; the default header remains unchanged. Partial-setup cleanup and recovery run at offsets 0, 3,001 and 60,000, including normal movement ticks and the session time invariant. Invalid offsets are rejected. No production access seam or gameplay implementation changed.

Fresh road routes have two edges of length 10 and a cached distance of 20. Rebuilding the same route after target retirement currently retains the previous cached distance accumulator: `ClearFast` resets the list count, while `AddList` resets only its item count. The replacement test checks the actual edges, junctions and their summed length and prints the cached distance for investigation. It does not accept accumulated distance as a behavior reference. This observed discrepancy needs defect disposition before the route-reuse baseline is complete.

After building tests, run `build/mingw-debug-exe/tests/matrixgame_ai_decision_tests.exe probe.ai.reused_route_distance` to assert that the reused route's cached distance still equals its edge sum of 20. Repeat with `mingw-release-exe`. Both currently fail with exit code 1 because the cached distance is 40. This opt-in correctness probe shares the actual target-retirement and route-rebuilding sequence above; it is deliberately excluded from CTest and does not treat the defect as an approved golden. A separately reviewed correction should promote the check into the regular suite.

Production-budget cases load two synthetic repair-equipped designs through `LoadAIRobotType`, with configured weapon strengths and prices. A base supplies capacity and an income forecast of three resources per second; no map time elapses. `PGCalcStat` initializes observations before `BuildRobot` runs. Queue emptiness, all four balances, build-history indices and RNG draw counts prove that these calls stop at the decision boundary. At balance 100, the weaker design is affordable only in the forecast; without an existing plan and without an immediately affordable alternative, the current algorithm does not start waiting. Successful purchase, assembly, delivery and adaptation to changing battlefield threat still need separate scenarios.

The tactical tests add synthetic enemies at known positions, then populate the production `CInfo` lists as their initial observations. `FirePL` performs its real nearest-target and friendly-line checks with production geometry. These tests do not establish how `GatherInfo` first discovers those enemies, fog-of-war equivalence, an armed firing solution, hit probability or damage. Selecting a blocked fallback target does not mean that the engine is allowed to fire through a friendly robot. The observed MoveTo order, selected target, lack of firing and the triggering test boundary are recorded in JSON decision records in CTest output. Trigger names describe the supplied observation or deletion; they are not claimed to be an internal reasoning log.

The memory tests distinguish current target pointers from historical fields. They never dereference a retired object. Ignore timing uses a separate production `CInfo` under the advancing session, so autonomous `GatherInfo` cannot refresh that particular test list. Capacity-overflow eviction is deliberately excluded: the current implementation uses overlapping `CopyMemory`, which requires separate defect handling before a portable expectation can be accepted.

The automatic-mode test uses a small test-only map subclass to set protected initial map inputs: reinforcement percentage and remaining cooldown. It is allocated with `HNew` and destroyed through its exact derived type. The fixture has no topology, robots or buildings and calls the production player-side `LogicTakt(10)` directly. This is a gate and RNG-consumption contract; it does not advance map time, load a demo map or demonstrate delivery. The seed probe verifies that the first chance draw enters the scan, then reseeds before the tested calls. No production access seam was added.

## Remaining baseline work

AI-01 still needs multi-region strategic decisions followed through movement and physical capture, uneven nonempty-team redistribution with defect disposition, and production through a completed purchase. Representative member loss, nearest-base retirement, strategic expansion into adjacent regions, three-region objective ties and empty-team splitting now have executable evidence. The fixture does not invent a leader identity or claim arbitrary team-loss recovery. Group-cache inconsistencies have bounded opt-in reproducers described below; they still need defect disposition before exact bookkeeping expectations are frozen. The reproduced route-distance reuse discrepancy also remains open.

The opt-in `probe.ai.uneven_team_redistribution` checks a separate correctness failure. Two safe Capture teams start with one and three robots and an initial clock of 3,001 ms. Both are eligible for balancing, but membership remains 1:3 instead of the expected 2:2. The donor-search loop in `TaktHL` leaves `u` equal to the configured team count; the subsequent difference reads `m_Team[u]` instead of the chosen donor `m_Team[k]`. The probe configures two active teams, so this erroneous read stays inside the allocated third record, whose initial robot count is checked as zero. It avoids executing the out-of-bounds three-team variant. Run `build/mingw-debug-exe/tests/matrixgame_ai_decision_tests.exe probe.ai.uneven_team_redistribution` and repeat with `mingw-release-exe`; both currently fail with exit code 1. This manual probe uses ParkMiller seed 79, is absent from CTest and does not accept incorrect balancing as a golden. A separate correction and regression disposition are required.

AI-02 still needs armed opportunistic fire and repair, capture priority against attached turrets, visual acquisition visibility/range thresholds, repeated failure to hit, resumption of intended movement after a genuine armed detour, and complete bomb-escape movement. Armed outcomes depend on faithful armed/repair fixtures and additional scenario evidence; the planning boundary and place-lookup defect now have separate evidence below. Radio propagation is covered separately from visual acquisition. Directly assigning a target or weapon result would not fill those gaps. Full-capacity ignore eviction still needs defect investigation because of overlapping copies. Selective cleanup and Reset have passing contracts; Clear reuse has a bounded correctness probe.

AI-03 still needs eligible-building selection, seeded robot choices, carrier requests and interruption, demo-map startup with AUTOMATIC_MODE/FULLAUTO/FLYCAM together, flying-camera/RNG effects and session transitions. The empty-building gate cannot establish these outcomes. Construction and navigation fixtures must supply the production assembly and carrier lifecycle before their automatic trigger can be tested end to end.

Historical decision references, fresh-process repetition, compiler and DLL comparisons, native evidence and performance measurements remain part of the complete baseline gate. A passing component decision suite is insufficient to authorize structural refactoring.

## Cleanup and bomb-escape decision boundaries

Three additional registered cases exercise independent production logic without assembled weapon or building models:

| Case | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `memory_cleanup` | `RemoveAllSlowely` removes marked sightings and clears their current target pointers, retaining unmarked sightings. `Reset` clears current targeting state while preserving enemy memory. `RemoveAllBuilding(skip)` preserves the nominated building and robot sightings, then removes remaining building sightings when called without an exception. Building records are never ticked |
| `place_bounds` | The diagonal place at movement cell (8,8) contains its four-cell square; right/bottom and map boundaries are excluded. Off-diagonal lookup is tested separately as a correctness failure |
| `bomb_escape_planning` | Ordinary player movement does not trigger escape. Automatic attack leaves the closest defender as cover and gives the other threatened robot an escape destination plus its original destination as a return order. A repeated call preserves that order. Blocking both outgoing chassis edges prevents escape; distances 299/300/301 distinguish the strict 300-world-unit threat radius |

Bomb planning equips one existing synthetic robot with the same idle production BIGBOOM effect used by the bomb-command tests. An RAII guard releases it before session teardown; no map tick, firing, animation or explosion runs while it is attached. The test supplies initial observations, invokes `EscapeFromBomb`, and checks that the decision consumes no RNG draws. This is not evidence for physical escape, weapon acquisition or blast avoidance during a battle.

The four-place planning fixture explicitly has no other reserved places or movement destinations. The normal escape queues its starting place and two neighbors before accepting the first neighbor: at most three indices in the four-entry production allocation. The blocked case uses one entry; the just-inside-radius case accepts its starting navigation place directly. Occupied-refuge scenarios remain excluded: `EscapeFromBomb` stores an occupied-place prefix and BFS visits in the same buffer, but `PrepareBuf` allocates only the larger of zone count and place count. This source-confirmed capacity concern requires separate investigation before executing crowded variants safely.

Four additional manual correctness probes are deliberately absent from CTest. After building, run `build/mingw-debug-exe/tests/matrixgame_ai_decision_tests.exe` with one name below; repeat from `mingw-release-exe`. Each currently exits with code 1. They assert intended consistency without freezing the erroneous result as a behavior reference:

| Probe | Observed discrepancy |
| --- | --- |
| `probe.ai.memory_clear_reuse` | `Clear` empties the linked list but leaves its count at 1. The first assertion fails before adding a new sighting; all referenced objects remain live |
| `probe.ai.place_axis` | Place (48,8) rejects its own origin, while place (8,48) accepts (20,48) outside its footprint. `FindPlace` compares the x coordinate against `tp.y + 4` |
| `probe.ai.regroup_split_counts` | Splitting two same-team robots 401 units apart produces one actual member in the original group but a cached count of 3 |
| `probe.ai.regroup_merge_counts` | Merging two nearby same-team groups leaves the empty donor with a cached count of 1 |

These probes inspect retained live objects and in-bounds records; they do not execute overflow paths. Their correction and promotion into registered regressions require separate defect disposition. No production implementation changed.
