# Strategic and tactical AI contracts

The `game.ai.*` suite includes decision and memory cases for [the AI behavior baseline](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/84), in addition to four robot-definition checks. They call production algorithms and use generated geometry where a decision needs positions, visibility or navigation. They do not replace the AI or change its priorities. The complete catalog remains open: the uncovered scenarios below are baseline blockers.

```powershell
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Debug -Filter '^game\.ai\.'
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Release -Filter '^game\.ai\.'
```

## Fixture boundaries

The shared `Session` retains its wheel-only synthetic map, active sides, fake audio service and storage-only D3D device. It uses seed 79, controlled map time and the declared no-draw floating-point profile. The integrated strategy case runs both RNG modes at 10 ms; it is not a resource-backed campaign or a native rendering check.

The priority tests populate a public candidate list and call the real comparison and reduction methods. `TaktHL` first initializes the real regional observations where they are needed. This characterizes the ranking contract without pretending that the fixture generated its candidates through a complete strategic expansion. The grouping tests establish initial positions, team IDs and group IDs before calling `Regroup`; they do not teleport robots during a battle. The threshold and action cases assert membership and retained action; the group-count cases separately compare every cached count with live membership.

The new regional cases partition the synthetic movement grid into left and right regions before allocating side observations. Four existing navigation places are assigned to those regions. The objective cases additionally create a four-junction, four-road graph with two equal routes between them. They call `PGFindAttackTarget`, `PGFindCaptureFactory` and `PGFindDefenceTarget` directly and assert the selected object, region sequence and road junctions. They do not advance a multi-region battle or demonstrate physical arrival, capture or combat. Factory and cannon records provide only the ownership, position, state and configured strength consumed by these algorithms; no building or turret geometry is loaded. The radio cases use initial sightings in `CInfo`; production `GatherInfo(1)` decides whether another robot can receive them. The separate armed cases exercise visual acquisition through `GatherInfo(0)`.

The additional strategic cases run both RNG modes with seed 79. Member retirement uses complete production map frames; base and objective cases call `TaktHL` without ticking their mesh-free building records. Bases have zero funds and no turret slots. Nearest-base selection is the current geometric region-center observation, not a guarantee of a traversable escape route. Objective ties use three regions connected as a star with two equal-length roads; each leaf contains one neutral factory. These checks establish strategic planning, not physical capture or arrival. The objective-removal case preserves the existing distinction between a region-level Forward plan and a live Capture opportunity.

The empty-team timer case uses a test-only initial clock input rather than simulating three seconds of prior gameplay. `Profile.initial_time_ms` defaults to zero, accepts 0 through 60,000, and is appended after the existing aggregate fields. A test-only map subclass sets the protected clock during setup and is destroyed through its exact derived type; subsequent time advances still run the production map tick. Nonzero offsets appear explicitly in scenario metadata; the default header remains unchanged. Partial-setup cleanup and recovery run at offsets 0, 3,001 and 60,000, including normal movement ticks and the session time invariant. Invalid offsets are rejected. No production access seam or gameplay implementation changed.

`game.ai.reused_route_distance` repeats the target-retirement and route-rebuilding sequence above and requires the reused route's cached distance to equal its edge sum of 20. It failed with cached distance 40 before the correction for [#138](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/138), which resets each new route list's distance when storage is reused. Route search order, graph topology and cache ownership are unchanged.

Production-budget cases load two synthetic repair-equipped designs through `LoadAIRobotType`, with configured weapon strengths and prices. A base supplies capacity and an income forecast of three resources per second; no map time elapses. `PGCalcStat` initializes observations before `BuildRobot` runs. Queue emptiness, all four balances, build-history indices and RNG draw counts prove that these calls stop at the decision boundary. At balance 100, the weaker design is affordable only in the forecast; without an existing plan and without an immediately affordable alternative, the current algorithm does not start waiting. Successful purchase, assembly, delivery and adaptation to changing battlefield threat still need separate scenarios.

The unarmed tactical tests add synthetic enemies at known positions, then populate the production `CInfo` lists as their initial observations. `FirePL` performs its real nearest-target and friendly-line checks with production geometry. These tests do not establish how `GatherInfo` first discovers those enemies, fog-of-war equivalence, an armed firing solution, hit probability or damage. Selecting a blocked fallback target does not mean that the engine is allowed to fire through a friendly robot. The observed MoveTo order, selected target, lack of firing and the triggering test boundary are recorded in JSON decision records in CTest output. Trigger names describe the supplied observation or deletion; they are not claimed to be an internal reasoning log.

The visual acquisition/retirement and armed intent/retention cases reuse the reviewed [armed fixture](armed-fixtures.md), run both RNG modes with seed 79 and call production decisions without advancing map time. Real weapon initialization supplies laser range 100 and plasma range 300; no private range fields or replacement targeting algorithm are used. Acquisition calls real visibility traces and reachable-place checks. The enemy-range case explicitly assembles an initially unarmed enemy between two observations; this is a controlled change of fixture input, not evidence of an in-game upgrade. The other range cases use separate initial scenes, and the retirement cases seed their declared historical sighting and current `m_Target`/`m_TargetAttack` pointers. The retained-pointer checks show that `GatherInfo` leaves those initial pointers untouched; miss counts and eventual retirement establish the hysteresis contract. Their rays are diagonal; axis-aligned visibility is covered by `game.combat.axis_aligned_trace`. These cases do not cover arbitrary terrain or building occlusion. The turret-awareness section below adds separate cannon visibility evidence. JSON records report observation/fire/movement state at each stated decision boundary; they are not replay traces of a complete battle. Armed `FirePL` checks establish issued fire orders, target ownership and preserved movement intent; projectile travel, damage and physical resumption after a detour are outside their scope.

The memory tests distinguish current target pointers from historical fields. They never dereference a retired object. Ignore timing uses a separate production `CInfo` under the advancing session, so autonomous `GatherInfo` cannot refresh that particular test list. The separate correction for [#137](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/137) adds registered Clear-reuse and full-capacity eviction tests described under Memory correction regressions.

The automatic-mode test uses a small test-only map subclass to set protected initial map inputs: reinforcement percentage and remaining cooldown. It is allocated with `HNew` and destroyed through its exact derived type. The fixture has no topology, robots or buildings and calls the production player-side `LogicTakt(10)` directly. This is a gate and RNG-consumption contract; it does not advance map time, load a demo map or demonstrate delivery. The seed probe verifies that the first chance draw enters the scan, then reseeds before the tested calls. No production access seam was added.

The three repair cases assemble a repair-only robot with two synthetic barrel attachment matrices, then use production `RobotWeaponInit`, `CalcRobotMass`, `RepairPL`, `PGOrderRepair` and `TaktPL`. Patients start at 50 internal hit points and receive one point of damage through `Damage`; a later production repair-health update changes target eligibility. Both RNG modes use seed 79. The range decision itself consumes no RNG. Checks distinguish the movement intention, current patient and fire order mode 2. These are bounded decision checks: no map time or beam impact is simulated, and the explicit health update is not evidence that a beam reaches a patient. Full-health and deleted patients are rejected without dereferencing retired objects. Decision records name the supplied trigger and report whether a current target and fire/movement orders remain.

The capture-priority cases create an enemy base and cannon through the shared generated building and armed geometry, with explicit parent/slot ownership and finite cannon health/strength. `PGOrderCapture` establishes the player intention; subsequent decisions run `TaktPL`, and `StaticDelete` performs guard retirement. Both RNG modes use seed 79. The newly observed cannon is an explicit input to target memory, so these cases isolate the response to recognition rather than claiming sensor acquisition. Decision records distinguish the retained group order, war flag, active capturer count and remaining guards. Map time does not advance: the scenarios establish interruption and resumption of capture orders, not movement, an actual guard kill or completed base capture.

Initial logic-list order matters. The production map loader registers buildings, robots and cannons in that order. The shared session already contains robots, so the capture fixture re-registers those robots through `DelLT`/`AddLT` during initial setup to put its base first, before adding the cannon. No scenario command has run at this point, and no ordering changes occur during the tested decisions. This follows the existing loader contract: the capture validator in `MatrixSide.cpp`, `CMatrixSideUnit::TaktPL` (the `mpo_Capture` branch around lines 7103-7117), stops scanning when it finds an existing capturer, so a synthetic base placed after that robot would not yet have been counted as a live objective. Runtime creation of buildings after existing capturers is outside this scenario.

## Correctness probes

Every opt-in AI correctness probe has been corrected and promoted to a registered `game.ai.*` regression; none remains outside CTest. The sections on individual contracts describe each correction.

The registered `game.ai.uneven_team_redistribution` regression covers the corrected donor index in `TaktHL` and immediate consistency of team membership and robot counts. Both RNG modes exercise one, two and three active teams, donor sizes of one, two, three and five, the 3,000/3,001 ms boundary, and the regroup-after-war exclusion. ## Turret awareness and memory

Seven `game.ai.turret_*` cases exercise the cannon branch of production `GatherInfo(0)` with both RNG modes and seed 84. A low-profile, three-piece synthetic turret uses 10-unit attachment heights so the production sight endpoint at ground + 20 intersects its bounding sphere. This is explicit fixture geometry; no visibility result or enemy acquisition is substituted. The scenarios call observation at fixed battle time rather than running a complete armed battle.

`turret_acquisition_range` brackets the acquisition limits for a 100-unit-range observer against 50- and 200-unit-range turrets. Horizontal distances 95/105 distinguish the observer-range case; 190/210 distinguish the longer turret-range case. Production includes the turret center's height in its distance test. A clear sight line is required before the first scan, and repeated scans cannot duplicate an entry. These samples bracket the limits; they do not assert exact floating-point equality at the threshold.

`turret_retirement_range` starts from a declared historical sighting outside acquisition range. For a 50-unit-range turret, horizontal distances 145/147 bracket the observer-dominated retirement limit; for a 200-unit-range turret, distances 297/299 bracket the turret-dominated limit. The nearer entry survives and the farther entry is removed after three scans. These samples distinguish three-dimensional distance and the cannon-specific 1.5 range factor. If the turret holds a retained core reference to the observer as its target, even the farther entry must survive with no pending-removal count. This target reference is fixture input to the memory decision, not evidence of autonomous turret aiming; production destruction releases it.

`turret_acquisition_filters` excludes friendly, unfinished and ignored turrets, then changes only that declared condition and requires acquisition. `turret_visual_obstruction` places a friendly robot across the sight line: observation must neither acquire nor ignore the hidden turret, and removal of the blocker must permit acquisition. The equality and elevation cases below add partial A6 range and terrain evidence. The separate 15-degree elevation rejection and its `AddIgnore` outcome remain untested; these shallow above/below scenes do not exercise that rejection. Autonomous turret aiming and firing remain covered separately by COM-03; these observation checks do not run complete combat outcomes.

### Exact range and elevation

`turret_acquisition_equality` and `turret_retirement_equality` place an armed observer on a flat platform, with the turret center 20 units above or below its center. The observer has range 100; turrets have range 50 or 200. Integer three-dimensional vectors give squared lengths of 12,100 and 40,804 for the acquisition samples (nominal radii 110 and 202), and exact retirement boundaries of 22,500 and 90,000 (radii 150 and 300). The 12,100 sample equals the production threshold only in the MinGW session's 24-bit x87 precision profile (`_PC_24`). ## Ineffective and obstructed fire boundaries

`ineffective_fire_decisions` supplies an occupied firing place and historical enemy memory to the production `WarPL` decision at 12,000 ms. A stationary robot with multiple enemies clears its attack target only when both target age and last-hit age exceed 4,000 ms. With one enemy, that condition instead marks its place as bad and invalidates the place. A target older than 2,000 ms with no hit for more than 10,000 ms also invalidates the place, including the multiple-enemy case. The current call still issues its fire order before these decisions; clearing attack intent does not immediately clear the fire target.

`blocked_fire_decisions` adds a real friendly robot across the firing line. No fire order is issued and the last-fire timestamp stays unchanged. With several enemies, target and last-fire ages strictly above 4,000 ms clear the target; a target older than 4,000 ms and last fire older than 6,000 ms invalidate the place only when more than 2,000 ms have elapsed since the last failed place search. Separate samples set that failure time to 10,000 and 9,999 ms: the exact 2,000 ms boundary retains the place, and the following millisecond permits abandonment. The supplied last-hit timestamp is current, so these checks distinguish blocked firing from missed shots. Conversely the unobstructed case begins with current last-fire history and stale last-hit history.

Both cases independently vary target age at 2,000/2,001/4,000/4,001 ms and feedback age at 4,000/4,001/6,000/6,001/10,000/10,001 ms, with one or two remembered enemies and both RNG modes (seed 84). `moving_fire_decisions` repeats both matrices with a pending production MoveTo order; the decision retains target/place and refreshes target-change time instead. These AI-02 component checks do not simulate the preceding missed shots, the next target search, relocation, or a full autonomous battle.

Observed quirk (Q): with one enemy, target age 4,001 ms and last-hit age 10,001 ms, both ineffective-fire abandonment rules run. The first records place 0 and clears it; the second records -1. ## Competing repairs and replacement movement

`repair_competing_retirement` and `repair_replacement_move` run two damaged friendly patients through production map ticks while a repair-equipped robot follows a player Move. The first patient starts within repair range; the second enters range along the journey. Both begin at 32/50 internal health. The competitor is created first in logic-list order, but starts outside repair range. The current patient receives two four-point pulses before retirement or the early replacement Move; a later replacement variant waits for the third pulse. The competitor has received none at either boundary.

Deleting the first patient through production removal clears its retained core and the repair target. The robot selects the second patient, delivers five pulses with the last clamped at maximum health, stops firing and arrives at the original destination. A replacement Move clears the friendly repair target and synchronously reacquires the first injured friendly within range in logic-list order. At the second pulse the competitor remains outside range, so the same patient is reacquired; retention keeps it selected even when the earlier-listed competitor enters range. At the third pulse both are within range, and a new Move switches immediately to the earlier-listed competitor. That patient finishes before the original patient is reacquired and completed. Both variants finish five pulses per patient, reach the new destination and clear firing. The range condition and target immediately after the command are asserted separately; these are different outcomes of reacquisition, not a rule that Move preserves the repair target.

Both RNG modes use seed 84, open synthetic terrain, unavailable audio, separate 10/16 ms schedules and a 25-second observation bound. ## Combat during Move and Patrol

Four combat journeys advance complete production map frames with a laser-equipped player robot at (100, 200), an unarmed stationary opponent at (240, 100), and an uninvolved opponent at (600, 600) to keep the battle running after the target dies. The target starts with 40 internal health units; the laser inflicts two per pulse, with a 160-unit radius and 100 ms cooldown. Targets, enemy memory and group combat flags are acquired through production logic. The cases assert damage pulses but do not establish hit/miss-clock history. Synthetic geometry, initial positions, fixed enemy speeds and weapon parameters are fixture inputs. The original four navigation places remain unchanged.

`moving_combat_continuation` issues the player's context Move to (500, 100). The robot fires while travelling, kills the target after 20 pulses, clears firing and continues toward its destination. Its group never enters War mode, and its low-level movement destination remains unchanged. This distinguishes the actual Move behavior from the catalog's proposed assumption that every such encounter requires an attack detour.

`patrol_combat_continuation` issues the production Patrol order toward (500, 100). Its existing initial leg targets (100, 100), while the group retains the requested outbound destination. Real perception enables War mode, but the target holds the only nearby navigation place. `WarPL` never assigns a firing place: the observer's `m_Place` stays negative throughout War, and failed searches set `m_PlaceNotFound`. The robot fires while finishing its pre-War leg. The first search fails at 210/240 ms for the 10/16 ms schedules. A second fails at 2310/2256 ms, before the target dies at 2600/2464 ms. The robot then reaches (100, 100) and stands idle before its outbound leg resumes at 4410/4272 ms. This is combat and continuation after failed place searches, not movement to a firing position.

`CanChangePlace` permits reassignment only when more than 2000 ms have elapsed since the last failed search. The tests check each repeated search and the observed resumption or redirection against that timestamp: strictly after 2000 ms, within one subsequent side-update interval. The 100 ms `TaktPL` cadence can be crossed by a simulation frame, so the upper observation allowance is 100 ms plus one frame. Each failure also checks the target's ownership of the nearby place; the continuation case requires two failures and an idle interval after the kill.

`patrol_combat_retirement` removes the target through production deletion after its second damaging pulse. This is a declared external intervention, distinct from the other cases' combat death. The retained object core, enemy memory and both target references must clear immediately; firing stops on subsequent frames. Patrol remains subject to the failed-search window and resumes at 2310/2256 ms, after the one search failure at 210/240 ms. No attack detour occurs before this deletion.

`patrol_combat_replacement` instead issues a context Move to (500, 400) after the second pulse, inside the same retry window. The context command creates a fresh logic group, changes intent and clears War synchronously, while the existing physical leg remains pending. Redirection occurs at 2300/2336 ms, after the failed-search timestamp plus 2000 ms. This delay is conditional on the failed search and command timing; it is not a general rule that a replacement Move delays physical obedience. The fire order already active during War survives the command and can continue damaging the stationary target. Those later pulses do not demonstrate renewed aiming after Move: suppressing Move-group fire renewal alone leaves this case passing. Disabling all `FirePL` calls is not an isolated control for that property, because it enables the `RepairPL` fallback. Subsequent frames must end the combat, clear firing and reach the new endpoint.

Each case runs both RNG modes with seed 84 and separate 10/16 ms schedules. Every frame checks group intent and the uninvolved opponent's unchanged health and position; every damaging pulse checks the receiver and exact health progression. Each run must acquire the target, move before its first hit and end the engagement within a 15-second journey bound. The Move cases must reach the exact destination and report arrival through the production place check. Patrol must enter an approach region of x > 490 with y within five units of the requested endpoint; it can reverse before coming to rest exactly at that point. The tests stop there, before a subsequent patrol leg could encounter the uninvolved opponent. Evidence includes acquisition of War, first damage, death/removal, failed-search counts and timestamps, the idle interval, command replacement, resumption/redirection, arrival/approach times, pulse counts and RNG draws.
