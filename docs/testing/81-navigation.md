# Navigation and local movement contracts

These tests extend the synthetic battle runner with production navigation, group movement and carrier lifetime checks. They implement part of [the navigation baseline](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/81); the remaining catalog variants below still block completion of that issue and the whole-game baseline. No production movement algorithm or gameplay rule changes.

## Running the suite

```powershell
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Debug -Filter '^game\.(navigation|scenario)\.'
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Release -Filter '^game\.(navigation|scenario)\.'
```

The `game.navigation.*` family uses production-backed cases. Assertions use `MG_CHECK` in both configurations. Both navigation executables check tracked allocation balance where instrumentation is enabled. The existing `game.scenario.*` cases verify that adding map variants leaves the original runner operational. All fixtures are generated from code; no game packages, display window or native audio device are needed. `group_speed_finite` is the registered regression for the nonfinite formation speed corrected in [#134](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/134).

## Geometry and execution

`BattleGeometry` builds the same 64-by-64 movement grid, flat terrain groups and storage-format chassis mesh used by the original controlled scenario. Its default central obstacle is unchanged. Additional layouts provide open ground, a vertical wall with a four-cell gap, a U-shaped dead end, a closed barrier and a connected multi-region map. The closed barrier has two disconnected zones and regions, with places linked only within their own side. The corridor's place links pass through its gap. The connected map retains the central obstacle, partitions its surrounding space into four quadrant zones and assigns the left/right zone pairs to two regions. Four places, four road junctions and four roads describe the unobstructed upper, lower and side connections. Road point paths, zone lists, adjacency widths and region membership are explicit fixture inputs. These are navigation fixtures, not substitutes for loading a production map package.

Local search contracts call `FindLocalPath`, `CanMoveFromTo`, `OptimizeMovePath` and `PlaceFindNear` directly on the production map. The region case constructs a separate five-region production `CMatrixRoadNetwork`: two equal alternatives connect regions 0 and 3, and region 4 is disconnected. Additional component cases exercise `FindPathInZone` and `FindPathFromRegionPath` on independently declared graphs. The connected multi-region cases then exercise movement through those hierarchy levels together, as described below.

Movement cases dispatch production context or group commands, then advance `CMatrixMapLogic::Takt` through the shared `Session`. Open-ground formation runs use both RNG modes and both 10 and 16 ms schedules; corridor, disconnected-region, opposing-flow, mixed-chassis and removed-blocker runs use both RNG modes at 10 ms. The seed is 79. The original runner's camera, fake audio completion policy and floating-point profile apply. No rendering occurs. Profiles are printed with revision, compiler and build options; nondefault worlds additionally declare `navigation_layout`, while the default central-obstacle header remains unchanged. Traces from different layouts must not be compared as the same scenario.

For opposing flow, setup adds two friendly wheel robots at (500, 200) and (540, 260), outside the original selection. The selected pair receives a context command to (500, 230); the additional robots receive separate `PGOrderMoveTo` commands to movement cells (8, 18) and (8, 24). Existing enemy robots remain present and their production AI continues to tick. The test checks that the opposing groups pass each other and finish on the intended sides, with finite coordinates and a nonzero minimum separation. It observes motion frames, not internal CPU work.

The mixed-chassis fixture adds one tracked robot at (100, 320) to the selected wheel group. Its configured speed is 0.3 world units per logic step, compared with 0.6 for wheels; rotation, structure and terrain coefficients are specified in the test. It uses the genuine track movement path and landscape-spot creation with the wheel fixture's synthetic spot resource. The test checks distinct maximum speeds, common logic-group membership, completion for all three robots and an empty tracked-robot order queue. This does not observe the commanded group's formation-speed regulation; that remains a separate NAV-02 variant. The separate `chassis_journeys` case covers open movement for all five chassis with synthetic foot and exhaust attachments; the mixed formation case does not establish that coverage. Hover/water restrictions and the remaining combined-terrain journeys are separate requirements.

The blocker case adds an unselected, unordered friendly wheel robot at (300, 220), then removes it through `StaticDelete` before frame 300 advances. Both selected robots must finish at their allocated positions. This exercises removal during navigation; it is not a combat death or wreck collision scenario.

## Evidence and measurement

### Open journeys for every chassis

`chassis_journeys` runs unarmed pneumatic, wheel, track, hovercraft and antigravity robots separately from (100, 200) to (500, 200) on open flat terrain. Each chassis uses both RNG modes with seed 81 and both 10 and 16 ms ticks: twenty profiles in total, with unavailable audio and no native drawing. All receive explicitly configured maximum speed 0.6, rotation speed 0.04, structure 1000, water coefficient 0.5 and uphill/downhill coefficients 0.7/2.5. Before construction, the other four chassis movement-speed slots are set to zero; the candidate's asserted maximum speed therefore detects reading another chassis's movement slot. Equal configured speeds provide a controlled input; these values do not represent original resource balance or a comparative chassis benchmark.

The fixture replaces the two initially selected friendly robots with the chosen chassis and supplies its selection, then dispatches the ordinary ground context command. The two unarmed computer robots retain their initial distant positions with maximum speed explicitly set to zero; their AI continues to tick, and the test checks that they do not move into the journey. Every frame checks the retained Move destination, live chassis identity, finite bounded coordinates and speed, flat-ground height and upright orientation (with the existing 0.00000012 tolerance for D3DX unit-vector normalization). Each journey must include samples with scalar `m_Speed` equal to 0.6 in its middle section, reach exactly (500, 200), empty the low-level order queue and remain at the destination for twenty more frames. Arrival is pinned at 7,520 ms for wheel, track, hovercraft and antigravity with either timestep; pneumatic arrival is 14,220 ms at 10 ms and 15,712 ms at 16 ms, identically in both RNG modes. Output identifies the profile and chassis, arrival time and movement sample counts. These are scenario expectations for the stated fixture, not accepted historical trajectory references.

Pneumatic movement requires attachment animation because production `LinkPneumatic` corrects the robot's position to keep the planted foot fixed. The generated storage-format mesh therefore has four movement frames, alternating left/right foot attachments at local X = -5/+5 and local Y cycling through 1.55, 0, -1.55, 0, with the right foot half a cycle apart. Each frame lasts 100 model milliseconds before production speed scaling; the reverse animation visits the same frames in reverse order. Production `BuildPneumaticData` derives the gait from this mesh. The journey checks that all four animation frames occur, and that both planted-foot and stride motion samples occur while the configured speed remains nonzero. The test restores the prior pneumatic catalog and frame count after simulation. This exercises real foot linking with declared synthetic gait data; it does not establish original-model animation or backward journey behavior.

The pneumatic result specifically characterizes the no-draw scheduler. `LinkPneumatic` uses the cached object matrix when planting a new foot. Here that matrix is refreshed through `JoinToGroup` and the 100 ms `CalcBounds` cache; the test never forces a per-frame refresh. Drawing a visible robot also refreshes its matrix, so the measured arrival rate does not describe a drawn robot. In this fixture scalar `m_Speed` stays at the configured value between planted and stride samples, while actual ground travel is slower. The exact arrival assertions preserve this cadence and detect changes to the synthetic gait period without claiming native animation equivalence.

Track and pneumatic footprints borrow the existing synthetic wheel spot resource. Hovercraft dust and antigravity exhaust have the shared synthetic effect catalog available; antigravity meshes provide production exhaust attachment IDs 10 and 11. Ordinary effect logic may run, but the test does not assert effect creation, attachment choice or retirement, and nothing is drawn. Native model alignment, dust, footprints and exhaust appearance remain presentation checks. The wheel ramp/reverse/lateral/water-slope cases below add settled-band evidence for those terrain variants. N3 still requires transition-response timing and the impassable destination produced by N1.

Temporary production mutations confirm the oracles: making `LinkPneumatic` return immediately loses the planted/stride observations, and inserting an antigravity kind check that suppresses X movement in the shared integration fails the progress/arrival requirement. The latter is a conditional fault in chassis-independent code, not an antigravity-specific production integrator. Reading wheel movement characteristics for antigravity fails the maximum-speed check, and doubling the pneumatic animation period fails its arrival-time check. All changes are reverted before ordinary validation.

### Integrated region and zone traversal

`multiregion_movement` moves the selected pair from the upper-left zone toward (500, 500), across the central obstacle's blocked direct line. Both robots visit the original region, an intermediate zone and the lower-right destination region; both report arrival with distinct positions. A subsequent order to (120, 400) brings them back into the left region. The outward phase runs for 16 seconds and the return phase for 15 seconds. `multiregion_road_guidance` first dispatches the same outward command, then supplies the group's initial road route through production `FindPathFromRegionPath`. The route contains the real upper road, its two point/zone entries and distance 40. The robot's normal `ZonePathCalc` selects its route-aware branch when navigating from zone 0 to non-adjacent zone 3. After the same 16-second outward phase, both robots arrive, the route remains associated with the group, and no zone access marks remain set. This proves that a supplied route does not disturb arrival; route following is not distinguished on this topology. Both robots visit zones 0, 2 and 3, rather than zone 1 of the supplied upper road. A route-sensitive topology and no-route control remain NAV-01 work.

Both robots first report arrival at 15,180 ms with 10 ms steps and 15,248 ms with 16 ms steps in the measured MSVC profile. The outward phase therefore uses a fixed 16-second boundary, divisible by both steps. It checks the whole phase and retains exact destination, completed-order, separation and finite-state requirements. The return order begins at 16,000 ms. These observed arrival times explain the bound; they are not universal compiler-independent trajectory references.

`unreachable_replacement` first requests movement across the closed barrier and verifies no arrival or displacement of the lead robot for three seconds. Replacing that order with (120, 400) then completes for both robots within 15 seconds, without leaving their original region. No terrain or graph connectivity is edited during these runs.

All three cases run both RNG modes at 10 and 16 ms. Durations round up to complete frames; a 15,000 ms phase takes 15,008 ms at the 16 ms step. Every completed frame checks finite positions and group speeds for every live robot, including the computer side, and checks the selected units' bounds and center-cell traversability. The tests record visited zone/region bits, samples with nonzero accumulated collision weight and samples where the rounded four-cell search footprint overlaps a blocked cell. The latter can occur at obstacle corners because physical movement applies circular collision correction rather than retaining the conservative square search footprint. These are observation counts, not internal search-work counters. Each phase also reports its start, end and the first map time when both selected robots report arrival, in milliseconds. Profiles and final states are printed; these cases do not yet provide persisted historical trajectory references.

### Finite group speed

`group_speed_finite` replays the three-command sequence that exposed division by zero in `CMatrixSideUnit::CalcMaxSpeed`: issue (500, 500) at time zero, replace it with (120, 400) at 400 ms, and issue (500, 100) at 15,400 ms, in both RNG modes at 10 and 16 ms. After subtracting the formation's minimum projection, the spread `maxpr` and the leading robot's projection could both equal the 180-unit slowdown threshold (`COLLIDE_BOT_R * 10`), so the speed factor evaluated `0 / 0`. Before the correction for [#134](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/134), the pinned MinGW Debug and Release builds produced a nonfinite speed on side 2 at 26,040 ms with ParkMiller seed 79 and 10 ms frames. Every frame requires finite positions and group speeds, and both return and final orders must complete. Whether a build reaches the exact boundary depends on its floating-point trajectory, so `formation_speed_boundaries` below establishes the boundary directly.

A spread that only reaches the threshold now leaves group speeds unreduced, as a smaller spread already did. Spreads above 180 keep the existing interpolation.

The additional full-map cases use the open layout, seed 79 and 10 ms steps. Their assertions and printed outcomes are component/scenario evidence, not persisted historical reference traces. `dense_flow` counts observations with a nonzero production collision count; that number is not an internal queue-expansion count or a timing measurement. The road fallback, collision thresholds and sinking flag are existing outcomes, not approved proposals to change gameplay.

Open-ground, corridor and disconnected movement cases write JSONL files under the selected build's `tests/navigation-traces` directory. Each file contains the build/profile header, frame-zero context command, every completed frame snapshot and a completion footer. Each boundary is flushed, so a failed run retains its last completed observation. Filenames encode layout, RNG mode, timestep and repetition. Open-ground cases run twice in the same process and compare every snapshot exactly; this detects leaked session state. Use `tools/compare-battle-traces.py` for comparisons between separately captured files, applying the comparison rules from [the time/RNG investigation](../research/76-battle-determinism/README.md). Do not treat same-build repetition as a historical golden trajectory or a native-render equivalence check.

The local route helper prints `route_points` and `reached_cells`. The latter counts initialized movement-grid cells whose production search label `m_Find` is nonnegative after the call. It is a read-only search-footprint measurement; it is not the number of queue expansions or elapsed CPU time. The obstacle case freezes 2,951 reached cells and the crowded-destination case freezes 2,693. It is suitable as a deterministic observable beside a benchmark, but internal per-frame replanning/avoidance work counters remain absent. Adding a production counter would require a separately reviewed minimal observation seam.

The public scenario's seed, headless policy and generated fixtures differ from a native battle. Historical traces, three fresh-process runs, cross-compiler comparisons, complete DLL validation and performance acceptance belong to the integrated baseline evidence. Passing this suite alone does not establish those results.

## Delivery flights and carrier damage

`carrier_drop_landing` remains the isolated release case: it declares an 80-world-unit release height on an attached robot, releases it through `Carry(nullptr)` and deletes the now-empty carrier. Every falling frame checks finite, nonincreasing height and unchanged horizontal position. The normal map scheduler changes the robot from Falling to SuccessfullyBuilt on ground contact at 970 ms with 10 ms frames or 976 ms with 16 ms frames, after which a new `PGOrderMoveTo` completes.

`delivery_flights` adds the actual `OrderFlyer` -> `ApplyOrder(FO_GIVE_BOT)` route using the shared [reinforcement resources](reinforcement-fixtures.md). Every session starts from open synthetic topology, seed 81 and the explicitly declared unavailable-icon-target profile. Four paired configurations use cardinal approach angles, distances 300/450, approach heights 100/160 and landing heights 40/70. Each runs both RNG modes at 10 and 16 ms. The caller requests place 3 directly; maintenance proposal selection is covered separately by the reinforcement suite. Robot construction, carrier trajectory, midpoint release, falling, ground contact, inherited attack intent and carrier retirement use ordinary map ticks without editing private trajectory state.

The assertions follow object-core leases before dereferencing the cargo or carrier, require finite coordinates, stationary horizontal position and nonincreasing height while falling, and establish the ordering release -> landing -> carrier retirement. Each declared profile freezes its measured release, landing and retirement frame in the test table. At the fixed 30-second boundary, the delivered robot must remain alive at exactly (500, 500) and report arrival after its inherited command; the carrier must have left the always-drawn list. This is unarmed movement following an attack intent, not evidence of armed targeting or damage after delivery. One rejected icon target is required for every assembled cargo.

`delivery_carrier_damage` applies production repair and lethal laser damage at three distinct lifecycle boundaries: while carrying at 1000 ms, immediately after midpoint release, and after the robot reaches the ground. Repair leaves carrier health unchanged. Lethal damage deletes the carrier, removes it from the always-drawn list and marks attached cargo for death. Map ticks retire that cargo; a previously released robot instead survives falling and completes its existing order. Synthetic combat assets and a scoped crater texture catalog support the original explosion, debris and attached-cargo death lifecycle. The damage call is direct, so this establishes the receiver/lifetime contract rather than projectile acquisition or collision with a flying target.

All flight resources are generated public fixtures: material wheel/Birex cargo and a body-only carrier with no rotors, weapons or stream artwork. The tests preserve the explicit icon-allocation failure path and do not claim native rendering or valid packaged carrier models. Configuration identities, final states and measured lifecycle boundaries are printed; these assertions do not supply accepted historical trajectory references.

### Neighboring deliveries and landing surfaces

`neighboring_delivery_landings` calls production `CMatrixBuilding::Maintenance` once with a 60-point robot proposal, which produces two carriers. Four connected landing places form a square with centers 40 world units apart. One already belongs to a stationary robot outside the landing area. A flat control permits the other three; a second profile excludes one candidate with its place movement mask and matching blocked movement-grid footprint. The third repeats that exclusion on a continuous terrain plane with gradient +0.25 along X. Both terrain triangles and vertex normals describe the plane; place coordinates, connections and movement masks are initial fixture data, not output from terrain loading or obstacle-mask generation.

Each request must consume five RNG draws, assign distinct unreserved landing places, preserve resources and establish the configured cooldown. The control chooses places 1 and 2; the excluded candidate changes that to places 2 and 3. An immediate repeated request must create no additional carrier or consume another draw. Every cargo inherits an Attack destination at its chosen place through the ordinary delivery path. Map ticks follow both payloads through attachment, release, falling, ground contact and movement to their respective destinations; the test does not issue replacement orders after delivery. Every falling sample requires unchanged horizontal position, nonincreasing height and no penetration below the declared plane beyond 0.001 world units. Each cargo must change from Falling to SuccessfullyBuilt at its recorded profile-specific landing tick. Those checks detect contact below the plane and premature contact above it. The landing tick's transform check establishes standing height after contact: production can refresh that transform before the test observes it, so its Z alone does not establish the contact threshold. Both carriers must retire after their cargo lands, and both robots must remain live, at their exact assigned coordinates, with nonoverlapping final centers by 30 seconds. Core leases guard lifetime observations before any object access.

The final cached transform must lie on the plane within 0.001 units and within one world unit per horizontal axis of the logical destination. Without drawing, movement refreshes the transform through `JoinToGroup`/`Decelerate` and the 100 ms `CalcBounds` cache; once stationary, the robot retains the last refreshed pose. Thus the offset includes motion since that refresh as well as the final snap. The one-unit per-axis tolerance is an empirical bound for these twelve profiles (the largest observed axis offset is about 0.78), not a general bound on the snap or on other scenarios. No observation forces a refresh. Visible robots also refresh their matrices through `BeforeDraw`, which this fixture does not exercise.

The case runs twelve independent profiles: both RNG modes at 10/16 ms for each surface. It prints the request profile and per-cargo release, landing, retirement and final-position observations. The caller is an unregistered factory, so factory income, autonomous build decisions and capture do not participate. The original friendly robot starts at (100,200); the opposing sentinel starts at (540,100), outside the declared approaches. Both retain zero configured maximum speed, and every tick requires their positions to remain unchanged, keeping incidental collisions with airborne cargo outside this landing scenario. Cargo uses the shared unarmed wheel/Birex construction; carriers remain body-only. These checks establish neighboring deliveries, candidate exclusion and sloped landing, while multipart carrier animation, mounted weapons, physical approach collisions, successful native icons and accepted historical trajectories remain separate requirements.

## Complete journeys on slopes

`sloped_terrain_journeys` supplies a continuous plane through both terrain triangles and matching vertex normals. After installing the plane, the fixture invalidates and refreshes the robot transform once through `RChange(MR_Matrix)` and `RNeed(MR_Matrix)`, replacing the cached height from the original flat map before any command. Subsequent observations read the cached production transform without refreshing it. A single wheel chassis receives the normal context movement command from (100,200) to (500,200); complete map ticks derive its tilt, speed and position. The flat control and gradients +0.25/-0.25 use the same open navigation masks, a declared base speed of 0.6, uphill cutoff 0.5 and downhill multiplier 3. Every tick checks finite live state, retained command/destination and the height stored in the object core against the plane, within 0.001 world units. Interior samples at x=240..360 compare the derived forward tilt with `s = gradient / sqrt(1 + gradient * gradient)` and the speed with `0.6 * (1 - s / 0.5)` uphill or `0.6 * (1 - 2 * s)` downhill. Both tolerances are 0.001: they allow the observed tilt-filter residue (below 0.0005) while detecting errors in the terrain normal magnitude. All journeys must reach exactly (500,200); elapsed time must order uphill > flat > downhill within each profile.

Both RNG modes and independent 10/16 ms schedules run with seed 81 and unavailable audio. The plane remains above water, the second friendly robot is removed before the command, and no robot orientation or movement outcome is injected after setup. This extends NAV-01/NAV-02 terrain evidence beyond the direct Seek arithmetic checks. It does not establish terrain-file loading, changing slope transitions, reverse travel on slopes, cross slopes, combined water/slope traversal, other chassis, impassable slopes, native rendering or a persisted reference trajectory.

## Shallow-water traversal

`shallow_water_traversal` compares a single wheel robot crossing the same open route on dry terrain and through a shallow strip. Synthetic flat cells covering world X [200, 400) have height -3 in the wet profile and 0 in the control. The generated navigation mask remains traversable in both cases; terrain loading and mask generation are not exercised. The robot receives the ordinary context Move command to (500, 200), with maximum speed 0.6 and water correction 0.5.

Production map ticks must retain the destination and keep the robot alive. Interior samples must not exceed the expected speed limit (0.3 wet, 0.6 dry), and at least one sample must reach that limit. Waypoint approach can shorten a movement step, so every sample is not required to have full speed. After leaving the strip, the robot must reach dry speed again, then arrive exactly at its requested destination. The wet run must take longer than its matching dry control. Surface height, finite positions and absence of the drowning flag are checked along the route.

Both RNG modes use seed 81 and 10/16 ms frames, with a 3,000-frame limit per run. Reported arrival times are observations for these profiles, not universal trajectory references. Exact per-profile timing acceptance across supported compilers remains part of the complete baseline; this case currently protects speed limits, dry-speed recovery and relative arrival ordering.
