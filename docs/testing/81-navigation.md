# Navigation and local movement contracts

These tests extend the synthetic battle runner with production navigation, group movement and carrier lifetime checks. They implement part of [the navigation baseline](https://github.com/pozitronik/MatrixGame/issues/81); the remaining catalog variants below still block completion of that issue and the whole-game baseline. No production movement algorithm or gameplay rule changes.

## Running the suite

```powershell
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Debug -Filter '^game\.(navigation|scenario)\.'
.\tools\test.ps1 -Configuration Release -Filter '^game\.(navigation|scenario)\.'
```

The `game.navigation.*` family has twenty-six named cases. Assertions use `MG_CHECK` in both configurations. Both navigation executables check tracked allocation balance where instrumentation is enabled. The existing `game.scenario.*` cases verify that adding map variants leaves the original runner operational. All fixtures are generated from code; no game packages, display window or native audio device are needed. A separate, manually invoked correctness probe below reproduces an unresolved nonfinite-speed defect and is not part of the passing CTest baseline.

## Geometry and execution

`BattleGeometry` builds the same 64-by-64 movement grid, flat terrain groups and storage-format chassis mesh used by the original controlled scenario. Its default central obstacle is unchanged. Additional layouts provide open ground, a vertical wall with a four-cell gap, a U-shaped dead end, a closed barrier and a connected multi-region map. The closed barrier has two disconnected zones and regions, with places linked only within their own side. The corridor's place links pass through its gap. The connected map retains the central obstacle, partitions its surrounding space into four quadrant zones and assigns the left/right zone pairs to two regions. Four places, four road junctions and four roads describe the unobstructed upper, lower and side connections. Road point paths, zone lists, adjacency widths and region membership are explicit fixture inputs. These are navigation fixtures, not substitutes for loading a production map package.

Local search contracts call `FindLocalPath`, `CanMoveFromTo`, `OptimizeMovePath` and `PlaceFindNear` directly on the production map. The region case constructs a separate five-region production `CMatrixRoadNetwork`: two equal alternatives connect regions 0 and 3, and region 4 is disconnected. Additional component cases exercise `FindPathInZone` and `FindPathFromRegionPath` on independently declared graphs. The connected multi-region cases then exercise movement through those hierarchy levels together, as described below.

Movement cases dispatch production context or group commands, then advance `CMatrixMapLogic::Takt` through the shared `Session`. Open-ground formation runs use both RNG modes and both 10 and 16 ms schedules; corridor, disconnected-region, opposing-flow, mixed-chassis and removed-blocker runs use both RNG modes at 10 ms. The seed is 79. The original runner's camera, fake audio completion policy and floating-point profile apply. No rendering occurs. Profiles are printed with revision, compiler, build options and `navigation_layout`; traces from different layouts must not be compared as the same scenario.

For opposing flow, setup adds two friendly wheel robots at (500, 200) and (540, 260), outside the original selection. The selected pair receives a context command to (500, 230); the additional robots receive separate `PGOrderMoveTo` commands to movement cells (8, 18) and (8, 24). Existing enemy robots remain present and their production AI continues to tick. The test checks that the opposing groups pass each other and finish on the intended sides, with finite coordinates and a nonzero minimum separation. It observes motion frames, not internal CPU work.

The mixed-chassis fixture adds one tracked robot at (100, 320) to the selected wheel group. Its configured speed is 0.3 world units per logic step, compared with 0.6 for wheels; rotation, structure and terrain coefficients are specified in the test. It uses the genuine track movement path and landscape-spot creation with the wheel fixture's synthetic spot resource. The test checks distinct maximum speeds, common logic-group membership, completion for all three robots and an empty tracked-robot order queue. This does not cover pneumatic foot attachment animation, antigravity flight, hover/water restrictions or slope traversal.

The blocker case adds an unselected, unordered friendly wheel robot at (300, 220), then removes it through `StaticDelete` before frame 300 advances. Both selected robots must finish at their allocated positions. This exercises removal during navigation; it is not a combat death or wreck collision scenario.

## Evidence and measurement

### Integrated region and zone traversal

`multiregion_movement` moves the selected pair from the upper-left zone toward (500, 500), across the central obstacle's blocked direct line. Both robots visit the original region, an intermediate zone and the lower-right destination region; both report arrival with distinct positions. A subsequent order to (120, 400) brings them back into the left region. The outward phase runs for 16 seconds and the return phase for 15 seconds. `multiregion_road_guidance` first dispatches the same outward command, then supplies the group's initial road route through production `FindPathFromRegionPath`. The route contains the real upper road, its two point/zone entries and distance 40. The robot's normal `ZonePathCalc` selects its route-aware branch when navigating from zone 0 to non-adjacent zone 3. After the same 16-second outward phase, both robots arrive, the route remains associated with the group, and no zone access marks remain set. This tests following a supplied route, not the strategic decision to request one.

The outward phase originally used 15 seconds. MSVC 19.44 Release CI showed the lead robot still executing its move at that boundary, approximately one world unit from the destination. A complementary MSVC 19.51 Release investigation reproduced the lead position exactly and followed its collision delay and normal end-of-path slowdown to completion: both robots first reported arrival at 15,180 ms with 10 ms steps and 15,248 ms with 16 ms steps, in both RNG modes. The corrected 16-second phase is fixed and divisible by both steps; it continues checking the entire phase and retains exact destination, completed-order, separation and finite-state requirements. The return order now starts at 16,000 ms. Timestep, RNG seed, floating-point profile and production movement remain unchanged. MSVC 19.51 is supplementary evidence; the supported MSVC CI lane validates its own compiler version.

`unreachable_replacement` first requests movement across the closed barrier and verifies no arrival or displacement of the lead robot for three seconds. Replacing that order with (120, 400) then completes for both robots within 15 seconds, without leaving their original region. No terrain or graph connectivity is edited during these runs.

All three cases run both RNG modes at 10 and 16 ms. Durations round up to complete frames; a 15,000 ms phase takes 15,008 ms at the 16 ms step. Every completed frame checks finite positions and group speeds for every live robot, including the computer side, and checks the selected units' bounds and center-cell traversability. The tests record visited zone/region bits, samples with nonzero accumulated collision weight and samples where the rounded four-cell search footprint overlaps a blocked cell. The latter can occur at obstacle corners because physical movement applies circular collision correction rather than retaining the conservative square search footprint. These are observation counts, not internal search-work counters. Each phase also reports its start, end and the first map time when both selected robots report arrival, in milliseconds. Profiles and final states are printed; these cases do not yet provide persisted historical trajectory references.

### Nonfinite group-speed correctness probe

An additional three-command sequence exposes division by zero in `CMatrixSideUnit::CalcMaxSpeed`: after subtracting the formation's minimum projection, `maxpr` and a robot's projection can both equal `COLLIDE_BOT_R * 10` (180). The speed adjustment then evaluates `0 / 0`. The probe asserts finite state at every frame and fails as soon as the nonfinite group speed appears. It does not accept the corrupted trajectory as a reference or wait for the subsequent death/effect failure.

After building the navigation tests with the normal helper, run this probe explicitly:

```powershell
Push-Location build/mingw-debug-exe/tests
try {
    .\matrixgame_navigation_tests.exe probe.navigation.group_speed_nonfinite
    Write-Host "Probe exit code: $LASTEXITCODE"
} finally { Pop-Location }
```

Repeat from `build/mingw-release-exe/tests` for Release. The current synthetic profile uses ParkMiller seed 79 and 10 ms frames: issue (500, 500) at time zero, replace it with (120, 400) at 400 ms, and issue (500, 100) at 15,400 ms. Both MinGW configurations reproduce nonfinite speed on side 2 at 26,040 ms, while the affected robot is still at finite coordinates (173.651, 464.732). The probe is intentionally not registered with CTest; its expected correctness result remains finite state, and the current failure requires a separately reviewed correction before this replacement sequence can enter the accepted baseline. Compiler-specific reproduction is still to be established.

### Additional route and movement boundaries

`navigation_boundary_tests.cpp` adds the following production-backed cases. Component fixtures supply initial topology, velocity, tilt or accumulated collision time explicitly and call the production decision once. Full-map cases advance the ordinary battle scheduler. These are distinct evidence types; initial collision weights are not elapsed battle time, and manually supplied tilt is not proof that terrain generates that orientation.

| Case suffix | Asserted behavior and scope |
| --- | --- |
| `zone_routes` | A five-zone graph chooses 0 -> 1 -> 3; a wheel-only restriction changes that to 0 -> 2 -> 3 while tracks retain the first path. An edge restriction removes the remaining wheel route. Identical/disconnected endpoints return zero; a later valid search succeeds without retained access marks. NAV-01 component evidence |
| `road_routes` | Road-junction search first avoids an unrelated region, selecting a route of declared distance 30 over an available distance-2 alternative. Blocking the first road permits the other-region route on the next search pass. When both roads block wheels, its final fallback ignores movement masks and returns the first route; local traversability is a separate decision. A disconnected destination clears the route. NAV-01 component evidence; this preserves existing search priorities rather than asserting shortest-path behavior |
| `chassis_traversal` | A full-width synthetic stripe blocks each of the five chassis bits in turn, across all declared footprints. `FindLocalPath` and `CanMoveFromTo` reject the blocked chassis and retain the exact 41-point direct path for the others. The masks are explicit initial data, not generated from private terrain resources. NAV-01 |
| `collision_boundaries` | The production spatial query and robot collision callback distinguish predicted separations 35/36/37 and 71/72/73. Under 36, a following robot stops and both receive 20 units of collision weight. Under 72, the follower's speed limit becomes 0.3; at 72 it remains 100. Positions remain unchanged because this case invokes the collision decision alone. NAV-02 |
| `collision_wait_threshold` | A stationary friendly blocker receives a return-and-yield order when the caller's accumulated collision weight reaches 1000, with the caller reset to zero. At 999 it receives no return order. The chosen yield destination differs from the blocker's current movement cell. NAV-02 |
| `moving_target` | A friendly live robot receives an ordinary movement order while the selected pair pursues it through an explicit attack intent, a supported existing command route. The target moves under production logic; the pursuer's allocated movement destination changes. Deleting the target clears its group reference, and a replacement ground move completes. Both RNG modes run independently. This unarmed friendly-target case establishes pursuit and recovery, not combat acquisition or friendly damage. NAV-01/NAV-02 |
| `replacement_near_blocker` | A zero-maximum-speed friendly robot obstructs the first journey; after 300 frames the selected pair receives a different destination and both complete at their assigned coordinates. The blocker remains live with zero maximum speed. This is an explicit speed constraint, not a paralysis effect or a wreck. Both RNG modes run independently. NAV-02 |
| `dense_flow` | Eight friendly robots receive independent opposing movement orders in four lanes. All eight pass and report arrival within 2500 frames; sampled positions remain finite and distinct. The case records nonzero collision samples and minimum separation in both RNG modes. NAV-02 |
| `terrain_speed` | `Z_From_Pos` supplies shallow-water state from a flat cell, while a declared chassis tilt isolates `Seek` arithmetic. Forward/backward travel, uphill slopes below/equal/above the configured limit, downhill acceleration and water multiplication have exact scalar-speed expectations. Direction checks allow 0.00000012 world units for D3DX normalization rounding; this tolerance does not relax the scalar speed or a trajectory comparison. No map tick or water effect is simulated here. NAV-02 |

The additional full-map cases use the open layout, seed 79 and 10 ms steps. Their assertions and printed outcomes are component/scenario evidence, not persisted historical reference traces. `dense_flow` counts observations with a nonzero production collision count; that number is not an internal queue-expansion count or a timing measurement. The road fallback, collision thresholds and sinking flag are existing outcomes, not approved proposals to change gameplay.

Open-ground, corridor and disconnected movement cases write JSONL files under the selected build's `tests/navigation-traces` directory. Each file contains the build/profile header, frame-zero context command, every completed frame snapshot and a completion footer. Each boundary is flushed, so a failed run retains its last completed observation. Filenames encode layout, RNG mode, timestep and repetition. Open-ground cases run twice in the same process and compare every snapshot exactly; this detects leaked session state. Use `tools/compare-battle-traces.py` for comparisons between separately captured files, applying the comparison rules from [the time/RNG investigation](../research/76-battle-determinism/README.md). Do not treat same-build repetition as a historical golden trajectory or a native-render equivalence check.

The local route helper prints `route_points` and `reached_cells`. The latter counts initialized movement-grid cells whose production search label `m_Find` is nonnegative after the call. It is a read-only search-footprint measurement; it is not the number of queue expansions or elapsed CPU time. The obstacle case freezes 2,951 reached cells and the crowded-destination case freezes 2,693. It is suitable as a deterministic observable beside a benchmark, but internal per-frame replanning/avoidance work counters remain absent. Adding a production counter would require a separately reviewed minimal observation seam.

The public scenario's seed, headless policy and generated fixtures differ from a native battle. Historical traces, three fresh-process runs, cross-compiler comparisons, complete DLL validation and performance acceptance belong to the integrated baseline evidence. Passing this suite alone does not establish those results.

## Delivery fixture still needed

The carrier's normal `ApplyOrder(FO_GIVE_BOT)` route constructs an `SSpecialBot`, generates textures, loads chassis/armor attachment geometry, calculates bounds and loads a flyer model before it can advance its trajectory. The current chassis fixture deliberately supplies neither the assembled robot catalog nor the flyer model/texture configuration. A faithful next extension must provide those synthetic resources and exercise the original creation route, coordinated with construction and combat fixtures. Calling `Carry(nullptr)` directly establishes payload state transitions; it cannot prove that a flying carrier reaches its release point. Bypassing the model load or setting the private trajectory position would not provide the missing baseline evidence.
